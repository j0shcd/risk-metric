# Tier evidence by data vintage and archive prospectively

Historical results will state whether they are Retrospective Reconstruction, Point-in-Time Eligible Evidence, or Prospective Evidence. Existing long-history analysis is retrospective wherever a required source lacks defensible availability dates or historical vintages; such results remain useful for research but cannot support “as known then” or live-style claims. Starting now, production runs will preserve compact provenance, model/config identity, inputs or content hashes, source availability assumptions, the DCA Risk output, and release identity before outcomes resolve so registered prospective evidence can accumulate.

## Consequences

- A result inherits the weakest evidence tier among its required sources.
- Unknown availability never silently defaults to observation time.
- Reduced point-in-time-eligible ablations should measure whether unverifiable sources add value.
- Current production scores may use presently available inputs; the evidence tier limits claims, not current computation.
- Prospective storage must remain free. Prefer compressed normalized observations, deduplication, and content hashes over duplicated bulky raw responses; monitor storage and operation budgets, fail before paid usage, and adjust retention or representation rather than silently entering a paid tier.
